% Chapter 29 of Genesis
\bookchapter{\Jacob Meets \Rachel}

\bverse So \Jacob went on his journey and came to the land of the people of the \location{East}.
\bverse And he looked, and was a well in the field; and behold, there \were three flocks of sheep lying by it; for out of that well they watered the flocks. A large stone \was on the well's mouth.
\bverse Now all the flocks would be gathered there; and they would roll the stone from the well's mouth, water the sheep, and put the stone back in its place on the well's mouth.
\bverse And \Jacob said to them, ``My brethren, where \are you from?'' And they said, ``We \are from \Haran.''
\bverse Then he said to them, ``Do you know \Laban the son of \name{Nahor}?'' And they said, ``We know him.''
\bverse So he said to them, ``Is he well?'' And they said, ``He \is well. And look, his daughter \Rachel is coming with the sheep.''
\bverse Then he said, ``Look, \textit{it is} still high day; \textit{it is} not time for the cattle to be gathered together. Water the sheep, and go and feed \them.''
\bverse But they said, ``We cannot until all the flocks are gathered together, and they have rolled the stone from the well's mouth; then we water the sheep.''
\bverse Now while he was still speaking with them, \Rachel came with her father's sheep, for she was a shepherdess.
\bverse And it came to pass, when \Jacob saw \Rachel the daughter of \Laban his mother's brother, and the sheep of \Laban his mother's brother, that \Jacob went near and rolled the stone from the well's mouth, and watered the flock of \Laban his mother's brother.
\bverse Then \Jacob kissed \Rachel, and lifted up his voice and wept.
\bverse And \Jacob told \Rachel that he \was her father's relative and that he \was \Rebekah's son. So she ran and told her father.
\bverse Then it came to pass, when \Laban heard the report about \Jacob his sister's son, that he ran to meet him, and embraced him and kissed him, and brought him to his house. So he told \Laban all these things.
\bverse And \Laban said to him, ``Surely you \are my bone and my flesh.'' And he stayed with him for a month.

\bmarkerdown{\Jacob Marries \Leah and \Rachel}

\bverse Then \Laban said to \Jacob, ``Because you \are my relative, should you therefore serve me for nothing? Tell me, what \textit{should} your wages \textit{be}?''
\bverse Now \Laban had two daughters: the name of the elder \was \Leah, and the name of the younger \was \Rachel.
\bverse \Leah's eyes \were delicate, but \Rachel was beautiful of form and appearance.
\bverse Now \Jacob loved \Rachel; so he said, ``I will serve you seven years for \Rachel your younger daughter.''
\bverse And \Laban said, ``\textit{It is} better that I give her to you than that I should give her to another man. Stay with me.''
\bverse So \Jacob served seven years for \Rachel, and they seemed \textit{only} a few days to him because of the love he had for her.
\bverse Then \Jacob said to \Laban, ``Give \textit{me} my wife, for my days are fulfilled, that I may go in to her.''
\bverse And \Laban gathered together all the men of the place and made a feast.
\bverse Now it came to pass in the evening, that he took \Leah his daughter and brought her to \Jacob; and he went in to her.
	\contextnote{}{The feast here could have likely meant that there was alcohol involved. Otherwise, how could \Jacob have not realized it was \Leah immediately? Perhaps it was just too dark and \Jacob could not see her visibly until the morning (\bref{Genesis 29:25}).}

\bverse And \Laban gave his maid \name{Zilpah} to his daughter \Leah \textit{as} a maid.
\bverse So it came to pass in the morning, that behold, it \was \Leah. And he said to \Laban, ``What is this you have done to me? Was it not for \Rachel that I served you? Why then have you deceived me?''
\bverse And \Laban said, ``It must not be done so in our country, to give the younger before the firstborn.
\bverse Fulfill her week, and we will give you this one also for the service which you will serve with me still another seven years.''
	\contextnote{}{Two things could potentially be at play here. First, it's been seven years and \Leah still hasn't married - perhaps \Laban saw this as an opportunity to remedy that. Second, the service that \Jacob provided could have been extremely beneficial and valuable, in which case \Laban would have wanted to keep him around and not lose that service.}

\bverse Then \Jacob did so and fulfilled her week\vmark{a}. So he gave him his daughter \Rachel as wife also.
	\contextnote{a}{This `fulfilled her week' phrase (also used in \bref{Genesis 29:27}) seems to be a dedicated time that was given to \Leah, after which \Jacob was given \Rachel also. The last phrase of \bref{Genesis 29:30} make it seem like this all happened \textit{before} the second seven years were served. Making \Jacob wait another seven years after the initial promise would have been a lot more awkward of a situation, and also \Rachel's age would have progressed another seven years, which may not have been as desired logistically (for children and such).}

\bverse And \Laban gave his maid \name{Bilhah} to his daughter \Rachel as a maid.
\bverse Then \Jacob also went in to \Rachel, and he also loved \Rachel more than \Leah. And he served with \Laban still another seven years.

\bmarkerdown{The Children of \Jacob}

\bverse When the \lord saw that \Leah \was hated\vmark{a}, He opened her womb; but \Rachel \was barren.
	\translationnote{a}{The NKJV Bible translates this to \textit{unloved}, however, the word (Strongs H8130 \cite{Strong's GodRules}) seems to be better understood as \text{hated}; almost as if an enemy.}
	\contextnote{}{This would have obviously been extremely difficult for \Leah. As suggested by the following verses, at this point it was entirely because of her Husbands \textit{lack of} love for her. She is clearly seeking this love in \bref{Genesis 29:32}.}

\bverse So \Leah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name \name{Reuben}; for she said, ``The \lord has surely looked on my affliction. Now therefore, my husband will love me.''
\bverse Then she conceived again and bore a son, and said, ``Because the \lord has heard that I \textit{am} hated, He has therefore given me this \textit{son} also.'' And she called his name \name{Simeon}.
