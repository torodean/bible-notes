% Chapter 27 of Genesis
\bookchapter{\Isaac Blesses \Jacob}

\bverse Now it came to pass, when \Isaac was old and his eyes were so dim that he could not see, that he called \Esau his older son and said to him, ``My son.'' And he answered him ``Here I am.''
\bverse Then he said, ``Behold now, I am old. I do not know the day of my death.
\bverse Now therefore, please take your weapons, your quiver and your bow, and go out to the field and hunt game for me.
	\generalnote{}{This could be used to suggest that hunting is okay, which is a common thing some people argue against in our modern culture.}
 
\bverse And make me savory food, such as I love, and bring \textit{it} to me that I may eat, that my soul may bless you before I die.''
\bverse Now \Rebekah was listening when \Isaac spoke to \Esau his son. And \Esau went to the field to hunt game and to bring \textit{it}.
\bverse So \Rebekah spoke to \Jacob her son, saying, ``Indeed I heard your father speak to \Esau your brother, saying
\bverse `Bring me game and make savory food for me, that I may eat it and bless you in the presence of the \lord before my death.'
\bverse Now therefore, my son, obey my voice according to what I command you.
\bverse Go now to the flock and bring me from there two choice kids of the goats, and I will make savory food from them for your father, such as he loves.
\bverse Then you shall take \textit{it} to your father, that he may eat \textit{it}, and that he may bless you before his death.''
	\contextnote{}{Earlier, in \bref{Genesis 25:28}, it is said that \Rebekah loves \Jacob. However, it is phrased in a way which suggests she does \textit{not} love \Esau. This could be why she is trying to undermine \Isaac's wishes here. Alongside this, it fits with \bref{Genesis 25:34}, where \Esau sold his birthright to \name{Jacob}, which could be fulfilled in this scenario.}

\bverse And \Jacob said to \Rebekah his mother, ``Look, \Esau my brother \textit{is} a hairy man, and I \textit{am} a smooth-\textit{skinned} man.
\bverse Perhaps my father will feel me, and I shall seem to be a deceiver to him; and I shall bring a curse on myself and not a blessing.''
	\bverse But his mother said to him, ``\textit{Let} your curse \textit{be} on me, my son; only obey my voice, and go, get \them for me.''
\bverse And he went and got \them and brought \them to his mother, and his mother made savory food, such as his father loved.
\bverse Then \Rebekah took the choice clothes of her elder son \Esau, which \were with her in the house, and put them on \Jacob her younger son.
\bverse And she put the skins of the kids of the goats on his hands and on the smooth part of his neck.
\bverse Then she gave the savory food and the bread, which she had prepared into the hand of her son \Jacob.
\bverse So he went to his father and said, ``My father.'' And he said, ``Here I am. Who \are you. my son?''
\bverse \Jacob said to his father, ``I \textit{am} \Esau your firstborn; I have done just as you told me; please arise, sit and eat of my game, that your soul may bless me.''
\bverse But \Isaac said to his son, ``How \textit{is it} that you have found \textit{it} so quickly, my son?'' And he said, ``Because the \lord your God brought \textit{it} to me.''
\bverse \Isaac said to \Jacob, ``Please come hear, that I may feel you, my son, whether you \are really my son \name{Esau} or not.''

\contextnote{}{\Esau was a very harry man, so \Isaac would be able to feel all of the hair on him to determine whether he was truly him.}

\bverse So \Jacob went near to \Isaac his father, and he felt him and said, ``The voice \textit{is} \Jacob's voice, but the hands \are the hands of \Esau.''
\bverse And he did not recognize him, because his hands were hairy like his brother \Esau's hands; so he blessed him.
\bverse Then he said, ``\textit{Are} you really my son \Esau?'' He said, ``I \textit{am}.''
\bverse He said, ``Bring \textit{it} near to me, and I will eat of my son's game, so that my soul may bless you.'' So he brought \textit{it} near to him, and he ate; and he brought him wine, and he drank.
\bverse Then his father \Isaac said to him, ``Come near now and kiss me, my son.''
\bverse And he came near and kissed him; and he smelled the smell of his clothing, and blessed him and said:
\begin{bquotation}
``Surely, the smell of my son \textit{Is} like the smell of a field Which the \lord has blessed. \bverse Therefore may God give you Of the dew of heaven, Of the fatness of the earth, And plenty of grain and wine. \bverse Let peoples serve you, And nations bow down to you. Be master over your brethren, And let your mother's sons bow down to you. Cursed \textit{be} everyone who curses you, And blessed \textit{be} those who bless you!''
\end{bquotation}

\bmarkerdown{\Esau's Lost Hope}

\bverse Now it happened, as soon as \Isaac had finished blessing \Jacob, and \Jacob had scarcely gone out from the presence of \Isaac his father, that \Esau his brother came in from his hunting.
\bverse He also had made savory food, and brought it to his father, and said to his father, ``Let my father arise and eat of his son's game, that your soul may bless me.''
\bverse And his father \Isaac said to him, ``Who \are you?'' So he said, ``I \textit{am} your son, your firstborn, \Esau.''
\bverse Then \Isaac trembled exceedingly, and said, ``Who? Where \is the one who hunted game and brought \textit{it} to me? I ate all \textit{of it} before you came, and I have blessed him--\textit{and} indeed he shall be blessed.''
\bverse When \Esau heard the words of his father, he cried with an exceedingly great and bitter cry, and said to his father, ``Bless me--me also, O my father!''
\bverse But he said, ``Your brother came with deceit and has taken away your blessing.''
\bverse And \textit{\Esau} said, ``Is he not rightly named \Jacob? For he has supplanted me these two times. He took away my birthright, and now look, he has taken away my blessing!'' And he said, ``Have you not reserved a blessing for me?''
\bverse Then \Isaac answered and said to \Esau, ``Indeed I have made him your master, and all his brethren have given to him as servants; with grain and wine I have sustained him. What shall I do now for you, my son?''
\bverse And \Esau said to his father, ``Have you only one blessing, my father? Bless me--me also, O my father!'' And \Esau lifted up his voice and wept.
\bverse Then \Isaac his father answered and said to him:
\begin{bquotation}
``Behold, your dwelling shall be of the fatness of the earth, And of the dew of heaven from above. \bverse By your sword you shall live, And you shall serve your brother; And it shall come to pass, when you become restless, That you shall break his yoke from your neck.''
\end{bquotation}

\bmarkerdown{\Jacob Escapes from \Esau}

\bverse So \Esau hated \Jacob because of the blessing with which his father blessed him, and \Esau said in his heart, ``The days of mourning for my father are at hand; then I will kill my brother \Jacob.''
\bverse And the words of \Esau her older son were told to \Rebekah. So she sent and called \Jacob her younger son, and said to him, ``Surely your brother \Esau comforts hilself concerning you by \textit{intending} to kill you.
\bverse Now therefore, my son, obey my voice: arise, flee to my brother \name{Laban} in \location{Haran}.
\bverse And stay with him a few days, until your brother's fury turns away,
\bverse until your brother's anger turns away from you, and he forgets what you have done to him; then I will send and bring you from there. Why should I be bereaved also of you both in one day?''
\bverse And \Rebekah said to \Isaac, ``I am weary of my life because of the daughters of \name{Heth}; if \Jacob takes a wife of the daughters of \name{Heth}, like these \textit{who are} the daughters of the land, what good will my life be to me?''
