% Chapter 28 of Genesis
\bookchapter{\Jacob Sent to His Uncle \Laban}

\bverse Then \Isaac called \Jacob and blessed him, and charged him, and said to him: ``You shall not take a wife from the daughters of \Canaan.
\bverse Arise, go to \PadanAram, to the house of \name{Bethuel} your mother's father; and take yourself a wife from there of the daughters of \Laban your mother's brother.
\begin{bquotation}
\bverse ``May God Almighty bless you, And make you fruitful and multiply you, That you may be an assembly of peoples; \bverse And give you the blessing of \Abraham, To you and your descendants with you, That you may inherit the land In which you are a stranger, Which God gave to \Abraham.''
\end{bquotation}
\bverse So \Isaac sent \Jacob away, and he went to \PadanAram, to \Laban the son of \name{Bethuel} the \people{Syrian}, the brother of \Rebekah, the mother of \Jacob and \Esau.

\bmarkerdown{\Esau Marries \name{Mahalath}}

\bverse \Esau saw that \Isaac had blessed \Jacob and sent him away to \PadanAram to take himself a wife from there, \textit{and that} as he blessed him he gave him a charge, saying, ``You shall not take a wife from the daughters of \Canaan,''
\bverse and that \Jacob had obeyed his father and his mother and had gone to \PadanAram.
\bverse Also \Esau saw that the daughters of \Canaan did not please his father \Isaac.
\bverse So \Esau went to \Ishmael and took \name{Mahalath} the daughter of \Ishmael, \Abraham's son, the sister of \name{Nebajoth}, to be his wife in addition to the wives he had.

\bmarkerdown{\Jacob's Vow at \Bethel}

\bverse Now \Jacob went out from \Beersheba and went toward \Haran.
\bverse So he came to a certain place and stayed there all night, because the sun had set. And he took one of the stones of that place and put it at his head, and he lay down in that place to sleep.
\bverse Then he dreamed, and behold, a ladder\vmark{a} \was set up on the earth, and its top reached to heaven; and there the angels of God were ascending and descending on it.
	\translationnote{a}{This word (Strong's H5551) is a literal \textit{ladder}. Could it be metaphorical here for something a bit more fancy - such as the angels here ascending and descending by something more sophisticated that they did not have a word for?}

\bverse And behold, the \lord stood above it and said: ``I \textit{am} the \lord God of \Abraham your father and the God of \Isaac; the land\vmark{a} on which you lie I will give to you and your descendants.
	\geographynote{a}{This is described as a \textit{certain place} between \Beersheba and \Haran. \Jacob names it \Bethel in \bref{Genesis 25:19}.}

\bverse Also your descendants shall be as the dust of the earth; you shall spread abroad to the west and the east, to the north and the south; and in you and in your seed all the families of the earth shall be blessed. 
\bverse Behold, I \textit{am} with you and will keep you wherever you go, and will bring you back to this land; for I will not leave you until I have done what I have spoken to you.''
\bverse Then \Jacob awoke from his sleep and said, ``Surely the \lord is in this place, and I did not know \textit{it}.''
\bverse And he was afraid and said, ``How awesome \is this place! This \is none other than the house of God, and this \is the gate of heaven!''
\bverse Then \Jacob rose early in the morning, and took the stone that he had put at his head, set it up as a pillar, and poured oil on top of it.
\bverse And he called the name of that place \Bethel; but the name of that city had been \location{Luz} previously.
\bverse Then \Jacob made a vow, saying, ``If God will be with me, and keep me in this way that I am going, and give me bread to eat and clothing to put on,
\bverse so that I come back to my father's house in peace, then the \lord shall be my God.
\bverse And this stone which I have set as a pillar shall be God's house, and of all that You give me I will surely give a tenth to You.''
